## 2. Section 3.4 – The Contingent Nature of the Universe
*(Reasoning from effect to cause; pp. 110–112)*

### 2.1 Core definition
- **Contingent (*hadith*)** = something that exists **after non-existence**. It had no prior existence, then came into being.
- The proof starts from the universe's contingency.
  - **Evidence:** the universe itself.
  - **Essence of the evidence:** its contingent nature.
  - **What it points to:** the universe's *neediness* for an **instantiating cause** (*muajid*) that brought it into existence.
  - **Indication:** the impossibility of the universe coming into existence without such a cause.
- The argument assumes the universe has an actual cause.
- **Big Bang cosmology** supports a universe that began (*ex nihilo*), even though some modern atheists resist this "brute fact."

### 2.2 Everything in the universe is moving or still (p. 111)
Movement, stillness, colours, life, the five senses and opposites are all proofs of contingency, because each comes into existence after non-existence.

If someone claims the universe or matter is **eternal**, one of three things must hold:

| # | Claim | Verdict |
|---|-------|---------|
| 1 | It has been **moving** for eternity | False |
| 2 | It has been **still** for eternity | False |
| 3 | It was **neither** moving nor still | False |

- **Why option 3 fails:** all matter fills a void, and anything filling a void is either moving or still. This is grasped by the **"eye of the mind"**, meaning intellectual perception of what the physical eye cannot see.

### 2.3 Why eternal stillness and eternal movement both fail (p. 112)
- **Eternal stillness:** if a universe still forever began to move, stillness would have ceased. An eternal thing cannot cease, so stillness was not eternal. Any change needs a **specifier** (*mukhassis*) to tip the scale from possible to actual.
- **Eternal movement:** we observe parts of the universe at rest, and movement ending also introduces a new, contingent state.
- **Moving from stillness to motion** (or the reverse) means adopting a contingent characteristic that did not exist before.

### 2.4 The additional caveat: moving bodies
- **Solid body** (*jism*), defined here as anything that fills a void, whether a rock or light.
- A moving body fills one void, then the next. Each stage is a **renewal**, so movement is made of contingent acts.
- Moving from point A to point B to point C means occupying different points, and each occupation is a contingent act.
- **Conclusion:** an eternally moving material universe cannot be eternal.
- The lecturer says the objection about reasoning *from part to whole* is addressed later (see [Section 9](#9-the-fallacy-of-composition)).

## 3. Section 3.5 – The Seven Principles
*(pp. 113–118; found in classical works, e.g. Imam al-Bajuri's commentary on the *Jawharat al-Tawhid* [?])*

**Result of the argument so far:** the whole universe is contingent. No part of the material world can be eternal, since every part is moving or still. The entire proof rests on seven foundations.

The lecturer quotes a saying that **the seven gates of hell are shut for one who knows these seven principles**, meaning they protect against misunderstanding that the universe has a beginning and that its cause is eternal (Allah).

**Running example: a planet.**

| # | Principle | Meaning | Planet example |
|---|-----------|---------|----------------|
| 1 | **Establishing something additional to the solids** | Bodies have attributes beyond bare existence. Nothing can be conceived with no attributes (even "clear" is an attribute). | A planet has a shape and a movement. |
| 2 | **Invalidating that the addition is self-established** | The attribute depends on its subject. | Orbital movement cannot exist without the planet. |
| 3 | **Invalidating that it transfers** | An attribute does not migrate from one body to another. | Jupiter's movement does not transfer to Earth. |
| 4 | **Invalidating that it hides and reappears** | If an attribute ends, it is gone, not "concealed." When it returns, it is newly originated. | A planet stops and later moves again: the motion is new, not "re-emerging." |
| 5 | **The eternal cannot perish** | If something could perish, it is only possible, not necessary, and possibilities are contingent. | If the "eternal" universe's stillness ends, it was never eternal. |
| 6 | **Material bodies never separate from these additions** | A body always has some state (moving or still). | A planet is never without movement or stillness. |
| 7 | **Contingents without a first are impossible** | A contingent by definition has a beginning, so a contingent with no starting point cannot exist. | See below. |

### 3.1 Elaborating Principle 7 (pp. 115–118)
**Objection:** "The universe is contingent, but maybe the chain of contingents goes back forever."

**Response (the chain argument):**
- Picture a chain of contingents numbered 1 to 10. Each link has a beginning, so the **chain as a whole has a beginning**.
- An everlasting chain of possible things is itself *possible* (its existence and non-existence are both conceivable), so it needs an **initiator**.
- **Sequence logic:** if A caused X, Y, Z, then A (being contingent) also needs a cause. It cannot be X, Y or Z, since A caused them. So the cause of the whole sequence must be **outside it**.
- Everything is **possible, impossible or necessary**:
  - An eternal chain of possibles has been shown impossible.
  - An *impossible* cause cannot explain anything.
  - So the cause must be **necessary**.

### 3.2 The Tallying Proof (*Burhan al-Tatbiq*) — pp. 116–118
- Attributed here to the kalam tradition and also presented by Ibn Sina (Avicenna) in *al-Shifa*, in his section on divinity **[?]**.
- **Mental exercise:**
  - Imagine two chains of contingent events. **Chain 1** ends at **Z**. **Chain 2** ends at **Y**, one event earlier.
  - Pair each event in chain 1 with an event in chain 2.
  - Chain 1 exceeds chain 2 by exactly one event (**Z**).
- **Result:**
  - Chain 2 has a limit (it ends), so it is **not eternal**.
  - Chain 1 exceeds chain 2 by a finite amount, so it too has a limit.
  - Therefore an **eternal regress of contingent events is impossible**. Anything that increases is not eternal, since the eternal by definition has no end.

### 3.3 Objection: set theory and infinite numbers (p. 118)
- **Objection:** Cantor's sets and transfinite numbers show actual infinity can exist.
- **Response:**
  - You can keep adding in the mind (multiplying by all the trees, stars and grains of sand, and then adding more), but such an "eternal number" has **no external existence** and cannot even be fully comprehended.
  - Sets and transfinite numbers are **constructs of the mind**. An infinite number of tasks can never be completed, so actual infinite sets cannot exist externally.
  - This point is attributed to Abd al-Aziz al-Farhari (commentary on *al-Nibras*) **[?]**.
- **Final point:** something that begins (like the number 1, the start of the number line) is not eternal in the way God is. **Paradise is everlasting but not eternal**: it has a beginning and is sustained by God forever, whereas God has no beginning, no end and is not sustained by anything. The lecturer calls this a basic point many people miss.
- Even if contingent events went on for an unimaginably long time, they would stop at a first event. Sustaining them still requires an **eternal, self-sustaining cause**.

## 4. Infinite Regress and Circular Reasoning
*(Section on *tasalsul* and *dawr*; pp. 120–121)*

### 4.1 Infinite regress (*tasalsul*)
- **Example:** the universe was caused by an alien life form. What caused that alien? Another alien? And that one? This produces an endless regress of contingents.
- **Impossible** because contingents by nature are originated from non-existence and so depend on an ultimate self-sufficient cause that resembles no contingent being.
- **Clarification:** what is impossible is a regress of **contingent causes**. The regress stops at the everlasting, necessary cause.

### 4.2 Circular reasoning (*dawr*)
- If A caused B and B caused A, each would depend on the other for its existence, so each must precede the other, which is absurd.
- It is a form of **begging the question** (*petitio principii*).

| Type | Example | Status |
|------|---------|--------|
| **Conceptual correlation** | Tom is John's father; John is Tom's son. Each concept requires the other. | **Fine** |
| **Simultaneous co-existence** | A heavy ball on a pillow and the dent in the pillow; a key turning at the same moment as the hand. | **Possible** (no one precedes the other) |
| **Causal/instantiating circularity** | A brings B into existence *and* B brings A into existence. | **Impossible** |

> **Rule:** the impossible circularity is about **causation / bringing into existence**.

### 4.3 Worked example: Hawking and Mlodinow (*The Grand Design*)
- **Their claims:** "Because there is a law such as gravity, the universe can and will create itself from nothing." And: M-theory predicts many universes created from nothing, which "does not require the intervention of some supernatural being or god."
- **The lecturer's critique (circularity):**
  - A law of nature depends for its own existence on the prior existence of the nature and matter it describes.
  - Gravity cannot exist before the universe, yet it is said to create the universe.
  - So the explanation presupposes what it must explain.
- He adds that "new atheism" accepts this uncritically, though it is theory, not rational or empirical fact, because it avoids God.

## 5. Proof from Possibility (Syllogisms)
*(pp. 122–126)*

### 5.1 Supporting verse
The lecturer cites the Qur'anic rhetorical question (Surat al-Insan, 76:1) **[verse reference restored from context]**: *"Has there not come upon man a period of time when he was something not mentioned?"* Humans once did not exist, so something brought them into existence.

### 5.2 Syllogism A: Originator
1. The universe exists after it did not exist.
2. Every existent after prior non-existence has an originator.
3. **Therefore, the universe has an originator.**

- To defend premise 1, you need the earlier proof that the universe is contingent.
- **Atheists typically deny premise 2** ("things can come to be without an originator").
- **Defence of premise 2:**
  - Something cannot originate itself, since non-existence cannot cause existence.
  - Its existence and non-existence are equally probable, so something external must tip the balance.
  - Appealing to a previous universe, previous humans or previous aliens leads back to regress (impossible), so there must be an ultimate non-contingent cause.

### 5.3 Syllogism B: Necessary existence of the Creator (p. 123)
1. If the creator of the universe did not exist **by necessity**, there would be no universe.
2. The universe exists.
3. **Therefore, a creator exists necessarily.**

**Why:** the creator is either impossible, possible or necessary.
- **Impossible:** then the universe could not exist, but it does.
- **Merely possible:** then he too needs a cause, leading to circularity or regress, which would entail our own non-existence. But we exist.
- **Necessary:** the only coherent option.

- **Bertrand Russell's** "the universe is just a brute fact" is described as a refusal to explain, not an explanation.

### 5.4 What the evidence signifies (p. 124)
- **Possible nature** points to a specifier. We could have had any kind of universe. The particular one that exists implies someone **specified** it.
  - **Analogy:** a scale with two pans. One must tip, but if it tips with nothing added, that is *tarjih bila murajjih* (preference without a preferrer), which is impossible.
- **Contingent nature** points to an originator.
- **Your own existence:** you did not create yourself. If you had, you could create lesser things, and your creation would have preceded your own existence.

### 5.5 "Nature" as a god-substitute
- **The lecturer's claim:** people now say "nature made this," but in doing so they ascribe **God's attributes to nature**. Nature is a *description* of what we observe and has no mind or will. The "greatest myth of our time" is giving nature divine attributes.
- Children are not taught to speak, yet they learn to.

### 5.6 Syllogism C: Specification implies will (p. 126)
1. One's very being is specified with particular features out of countless other possibilities.
2. Whatever is specified implies the specifier has **will**.
3. **Therefore, the one who specified your being is a willing agent ("doer of what He wills").**

- **Illustration (Imam al-Sanusi [?]):** if growth had no specifier, everything would grow into a **ball** (equal on all sides). Instead, plants, fetuses and children grow in specific forms (height, breadth, features), which implies a willing specifier.

## 6. Lawrence Krauss – *A Universe from Nothing*

- **Reception:** hailed in "new atheism" circles. Richard Dawkins's afterword claimed it did for cosmology what Darwin's *Origin of Species* did for biology, and said the "last trump card" (*why is there something rather than nothing?*) "shrivels."
- **The lecturer's response:**
  1. **"Nothing" is not really nothing.** Genuine nothingness has no requirements (no space, no laws).
  2. Krauss's "nothing" is a **quantum vacuum** that needs empty space and the laws of quantum mechanics/gravity.
  3. Krauss ultimately **pushes the question back**, invoking a **multiverse**, and admits he does not know where the laws of quantum mechanics came from. The lecturer paraphrases this as a near end-of-book admission (the exact wording is not quoted here).
  4. A "nothing" with a vacuum is still *something*, so we return to the contingency and possibility arguments.
  5. If it truly is *absolute* nothing, then the claim is that the universe has no cause, which contradicts the causal principle. And if "nothing" ultimately means a self-sufficient cause, the universe comes from God.
- The lecturer compares the wordplay to **Alice in Wonderland**, as the term is redefined to suit the thesis.
- The lecturer's own usage: when we say "from nothing," we mean from non-existence **brought about by a primary cause**, not "without cause."
- *Aside:* the lecturer jokes that you could waste a summer reading it.

### 7.2 "Assault on reason"
- **Definition:** rejecting **common-sense first principles**.
- Principles said to be rejected in practice: **causality**, **sufficient reason**, **the law of the excluded middle**, **non-contradiction**.
- The Big Bang debate between theism and atheism ends at causality: *does the Big Bang have a cause?*
- **A clarification on the excluded middle:** some say Allah's attributes being "neither Him nor other than Him" (*la huwa wa la ghayruh*) violates the law of the excluded middle. The lecturer says this is wrong, because "not other than Him" means **not separable from Him**.

### 7.3 Occam's razor (principle of parsimony)
- William of Ockham (d. 1342), a nominalist.
- **Principle:** simpler explanations are generally better.
- **Application:** one eternal cause is simpler than a complicated multiverse.
- The lecturer notes that atheists often accuse theists of "obfuscating," when the obfuscation lies in elaborate multiverse explanations.

### 7.4 The Principle of Sufficient Reason (PSR) — section 3.14
- **Leibniz:** no fact is true or existent without a sufficient reason why it is so and not otherwise, even if we cannot always know the reason.
- **In short:** *nothing exists without a reason.* Intuitive and compelling (links to the intuitive knowledge discussed in the Epistemology chapter).
- **Schopenhauer** distinguished four applications, one concerning change in the physical world.
- **The lecturer's claim:** atheists reject it in practice.

*(Section 3.6; pp. 93, 133–134)*

### 8.1 Hume's classification (p. 93, from Chapter 2)

| Hume | Kant's term | Features |
|------|-------------|----------|
| **Relations of ideas** (algebra, arithmetic, geometry, intuitive/demonstrable truths) | **Analytic propositions** | Predicate is already contained in the subject; known by the mind |
| **Matters of fact** (sense observation, memory of it) | **Synthetic propositions** | Predicate is not contained in the subject |

### 8.2 The Islamic position on causality (p. 133)
- It is **epistemically necessary and self-evident** that anything contingent (not existing before coming into existence) has a cause. This is known intuitively.
- Denial of this principle is held to be an **assault on reason**.

### 8.3 Hume and Kant's challenge
- **Kant's phrase (garbled in the transcript):** the causal principle is described as an illegitimate offspring of the imagination, "impregnated by experience." *(The transcript wrongly renders this as a proper name; the meaning is clear from context.)*
  - **Meaning:** our daily experience *makes us think* things are caused, but that does not establish it as fact.
- **Hume:** a "cause" is one object followed by another, where all objects similar to the first are followed by objects similar to the second. This is the **billiard-ball** example from the previous session. The necessity of cause and effect is only **hypothetical/conditional**.
- So for empiricists, the **laws of nature are not necessary truths**.

### 8.4 Where the Islamic view agrees and where it departs
- **Common ground:** in Islamic theology, cause and effect is **created by Allah**, so breaking it (a **miracle**) is possible. This links to *empirical/habitual judgments* (*hukm adi*) from Chapter 2.
- **Departure:** we affirm the universe has an **ultimate cause** (the cause of all causes).
- **Practical point against "brute fact":** treating the universe as brute fact would **impede scientific inquiry**, as people would stop investigating.

## 9. The Fallacy of Composition
*(Section 3.7; p. 132)*

- **The fallacy:** inferring a property of a whole from its parts (or vice versa) is sometimes invalid.
  - *Composition:* "These athletes are strong, so the team is strong."
  - *Division:* "This rope is strong, so every strand is strong."
- **The objection (Russell, Hume):** concluding the universe is contingent *because its parts are* commits the fallacy.

### The lecturer's replies
1. **Intuition, not mere induction:** through the "eye of the mind" we know the universe as a whole is either moving or still, and both are contingent.
2. **Composition argument:** the universe is a **composite**, and every composite depends on its components, so it has a dependent existence, which is the very meaning of contingency.
3. **Valid vs. invalid part-to-whole inferences:**
   - *Invalid:* "The airplane is made of small bits of metal, so the airplane is small."
   - *Valid:* "The airplane is made of metal, so the airplane is metal."
   - "The universe is composed of contingents, so the universe is contingent" is the **valid** type.
4. **Russell's alternative** (each thing contingent but the whole necessary) conflicts with **Big Bang cosmology**, which gives the universe a beginning (cited as roughly 13.7 billion years ago). Anything that begins is contingent by definition. Observable expansion is also characteristic of contingents.
5. **Hume's objection about a "necessary being":** it cannot be shown through sensory experience.
   - **Reply:** the claim is about **metaphysical necessity**, meaning beyond sensory perception. First the existence of a metaphysical being is established, then one discusses its attributes and distinguishes essential from accidental properties.
- **Study advice from the lecturer:** many objections raised by people who say "the book doesn't address this" are actually answered in the text, so read the relevant sections.

## 10. Anthropic Principle, Teleology, God-of-the-Gaps, Alien-of-the-Gaps
*(Sections 3.17–3.19; pp. 135–142)*

### 10.1 Cumulative argument
- **Fine-tuning, intelligibility of the cosmos, beauty, love, consciousness, the simplicity of mathematics, morality, justice, truth** all point to God.
- Atheists cite **evil** against God, so theists can cite all of the above.
- Together they form a **cumulative argument**.
- **But the lecturer insists:** God's existence does **not** depend on a cumulative case.
  - It is **self-evident** and part of human nature (*fitra*).
  - Considered "theoretical" only relative to some people.
  - A single argument (creation points to a creator; contingency points to a creator) suffices for some.
  - The anthropic and teleological arguments are **additional**.

### 10.2 Teleological argument
- From the Greek *telos*, meaning end, goal or purpose.
- Not all teleological arguments presuppose intelligent design. Some analyse **function** (as in biology and economics).
- Some use teleology to argue for a **fine-tuned universe**.

### 10.3 God-of-the-gaps (section 3.18)
- **The fallacy:** filling gaps in scientific knowledge with "God did it."
- **The lecturer's position:**
  - It is **not a scientific explanation**. Science (as covered in the Epistemology chapter) covers the material world through experimentation and description.
  - Muslims **should not use** God-of-the-gaps.
- Section 3.18 is mostly about the **fine-tuned universe**. Popular science books are recommended for deepening awe at creation.

### 10.4 Alien-of-the-gaps (section 3.19, pp. 140–141)
- **Claim:** atheists commit their own version, filling gaps with aliens.
- **Francis Crick** (co-discoverer of DNA's structure), in *Life Itself: Its Origin and Nature*, proposed **panspermia**: life seeded on Earth by advanced extraterrestrial beings in spaceships.
  - The lecturer's reading: Crick found that life could not arise from lifeless chemicals under any earthly conditions, so he needed "something other than God."
- **Richard Dawkins**, asked in an interview about intelligent design, said it is conceivable that an earlier civilisation evolved by Darwinian means, reached high technology and seeded life here. It might leave a "signature" in our chemistry.
- **Critique:**
  - This merely **regresses** the question (who designed the aliens?). It is a standard continuous regress, not science.
  - A theist reasoning this way would be ridiculed.
  - The lecturer says a later chapter (Ch. 5) discusses a Dawkins interview with Mehdi Hasan.
- **The lecturer's comment on culture:** popular films, books and music normalise aliens as "possible."
- **Link to Islam:** the lecturer notes the story of Adam as being not from Earth in origin (wording here avoids the word "alien").

### 10.5 Anthony Flew (p. 142)
- Moved from atheism to accepting a creator, citing the **integrated complexity** of the universe (components so intertwined that a malfunction in any would make life impossible).
- Per the lecturer, in an interview Flew affirmed attributes that match some of the 20 attributes covered earlier, but rejected the Islamic conception for **cultural** reasons.

## 12. Chapter 4 – The Problem of Evil

### 12.1 Epigraphs (from Arthur Conan Doyle's Sherlock Holmes)
- *"The Veiled Lodger":* the example of patient suffering is itself a precious lesson to an impatient world.
- *"The Cardboard Box":* Holmes asks what object is served by this circle of misery, violence and fear, concluding that it must tend to some end or the universe is ruled by chance, which is "unthinkable." The lecturer adds that human reason cannot answer this, but **revelation (the Qur'an)** does.

### 12.2 Framing
- Some call evil the **best argument against God**, which the lecturer says reveals the weakness of the arguments against God.
- It has deep **emotional and psychological** dimensions.
- **Key questions:** *How is evil defined, and who defines it?*
  - Evil is relative unless defined by the Creator.
  - **Example (cited to Ibn Arabi [?]):** a snake's poison is good for the snake and bad for other creatures, so is it good or bad?
  - **The mind alone** is not sufficient to determine good and bad.
- **Resolution:** only Allah defines moral good and evil. His actions cannot be called evil, because they are manifestations of His many names.
- **Definition used:** *evil is taking that which does not belong to you.* Since everything belongs to Allah, His taking cannot be evil.

### 12.3 The atheist's syllogism (p. 152)

> **P1.** If God exists, He is omnipotent, omniscient and morally perfect.
> - Omnipotent: He can eliminate all evil.
> - Omniscient: He knows when evil exists.
> - Morally perfect: He desires to eliminate all evil.
>
> **P2.** Evil exists.
>
> **Therefore:** God does not exist (or lacks power, knowledge or desire).

### 12.4 Flaws identified by the lecturer

| Flaw | Explanation |
|------|-------------|
| **"Morally perfect" is imported** | Who defines morality: humans or God? Obligations on humans do not apply to Allah. Christian conceptions of "moral perfection" are not the Muslim conception. |
| **Ignores free will** | Allah gave humans a **limited free will** for which they are judged. Evil in the sense of wrongdoing arises from human choice. |
| **Ignores the full set of names** | Mercy is only one manifestation. |
| **Ignores the Hereafter** | The world is not the whole picture. Without a **Day of Judgment**, the doer of good and the doer of evil would die equally. The Day of Judgment is a *logical conclusion* where the distinction is made. |

- **Natural events** (plague, disaster, illness in a child) are created by Allah and so, by definition, not "evil" in the sense of wrongdoing. What is called evil is **human wrongdoing**.
- **Why doesn't God intervene?** The lecturer says God does intervene often, but not always, and the question ignores free will and the Day of Judgment.

### 12.5 Stephen Fry interview (sections 4.2–4.3; p. 154)
- Stephen Fry, interviewed by **Gay Byrne** on Irish television, made a now-famous statement against God centred on suffering.
- **Main fallacies identified:**
  1. **Judging God by human moral standards.**
  2. **Ignoring most of God's attributes** (he mentions only "all-kind" and "all-powerful").
  3. **Overlooking human responsibility:** the lecturer argues much human misery is self-caused and outweighs "natural" misery (his assertion).
  4. **Misunderstanding servitude** (the "on our knees all our lives" ridicule): in Islam, **submission is the meaning of Islam**, and *kufr* involves covering over/denying one's Creator.
  5. **Salvation is by God's mercy**, which even encompasses the people of Hellfire in various ways.
- The lecturer stresses that the **entire chapter** is the response, not just p. 154.

## 13. Divine Power (Qudra) and the Boulder Question
*(Sections 4.4–4.5; pp. 155–158)*

### 13.1 Three ways an attribute connects to the external world (p. 155)

| Connection | Meaning | Example |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| **Signification** (*dalala*) | Signals something | Speech (*kalam*) conveys information |
| **Uncovering** (*kashf*) | Reveals facts | Sight, hearing, **knowledge** |
| **Effectuation** (*ta'thir*) | Brings about an effect | **Power** (*qudra*) breaking or creating |

### 13.2 What does omnipotence relate to? (p. 156)
- Divine power relates only to the **rationally possible** (*mumkin*), not to the necessary or the impossible.
  - **Necessary:** if power could change it, it would no longer be necessary.
  - **Impossible:** if power could make it real, it would no longer be impossible.
- A rationally impossible thing has **no real existence**, only a confusion of concepts (e.g. a **circular triangle**).

### 13.3 The boulder question
- *"Can God create a boulder so big He cannot lift it?"*
- **Answer:** the question is **fallacious**. It really asks whether God can make Himself **impotent**, which is an impossibility. Saying God's power does not relate to impossibilities is not a limitation on God.
- A reference to the story *Love Is a Fallacy* is made, which discusses this type of fallacy.
- The lecturer mentions a recorded lecture series on the *Bahin*-type text **[?]** and an unreleased single-sitting lecture.

### 13.4 The Satan-and-eggshell anecdote (p. 158)
- **Story:** while the prophet Idris (AS) was sewing and glorifying God, Satan brought an eggshell and asked if God could fit the world inside. Idris replied that God could put the universe in the eye of a needle, and then poked Satan's eye with the needle, signifying Satan's lack of spiritual insight (*basira*).
- **Source status:** found in commentary literature (al-Dasuqi is cited), but **not found with a chain in hadith** by the lecturer, so it is an anecdote.
- **Apparent conundrum:** it seems to say God can do the rationally impossible.
- **Responses:**
  1. **Ash'ari-school answer:** Idris meant God could **shrink the universe or enlarge the egg**, both of which are *possible*. He shortened his answer because the meaning was evident and Satan was not a sincere seeker.
  2. **Different universes:** some scholars (e.g. Ibn Arabi, through *kashf*) mention universes with different physical laws.

### 13.5 Intrinsic vs. extrinsic necessity/impossibility (section 4.7)

| | Intrinsic (rational) | Extrinsic (created/habitual) |
|--|---------------------|------------------------------|
| **Nature** | Cannot change, determined by the mind | Set by God's creation |
| **Example** | 1 + 1 = 2 (no universe where it equals 3); circular triangle | A tree needing roots (God could create one without) |
| **Relation to power** | Outside it | Within it |

- This distinction clarifies reports of universes with different laws without violating rational impossibility.

## 14. Divine Knowledge (Ilm) and Human Free Will
*(Sections 4.6 and 4.8; p. 160)*

- **Knowledge** relates to the **possible, the impossible and the necessary**, because it is an attribute of *uncovering*, not *effectuation*.
  - Knowledge of the necessary includes God's knowledge of Himself and His attributes, and since those are eternal, so is that knowledge.
- **Power vs. knowledge:** power relates only to possibilities. Knowledge relates to all three categories.
- **Free will:**
  - God's **knowing** that someone will do an act does **not** force the act.
  - Humans have a **limited power to acquire (*kasb*)** their deeds.
  - This links to reward and punishment, and to Hellfire.
- **Soft determinism:** some describe this view as soft determinism: all is determined by Allah, yet the human has a scope of choice.
- **Intellectual style:** the lecturer criticises "black-and-white thinking," which he links to extremism (the *Khawarij* mentioned).
- **Open questions** previewed for the next session:
  - Does human free will undermine God's will, or vice versa?
  - Does saying *insha'Allah* undermine human choice?
  - What does "Allah guides whom He wills and misguides whom He wills" mean for human choice?

## 15. The Ten Conditions of Legal Responsibility (Taklif)
*(Section 4.9; pp. 162–165)*

**Key concept:** **Mukallaf** = a legally responsible person addressed by God's commands and held accountable for choices. **Taklif** = legal responsibility.

**Why it matters:**
- Salvation judgments should not be made in "black and white" terms by bigots.
- Humans judge by the **outward** (*zahir*), per the Shari'a (e.g. funeral prayers are not offered for non-Muslims).
- The **reality** (*batin*) is known only to Allah.
- Going beyond the Shari'a boundary on this leads to *zandaqa* (heresy).
- If all ten conditions are met and the person is still sinful or an unbeliever, the ultimate judgment is God's.

| # | Condition | Details |
|---|-----------|---------|
| 1 | **Sound intellect** | Excludes the insane or those unable to rationalise belief. Such people have salvation and face no punishment. Taklif varies by degree of understanding. |
| | *Sub-case:* sane then insane | Judged by misdeeds committed while sane, if no repentance beforehand (other conditions permitting). |
| 2 | **Absence of intoxication** | Self-induced intoxication is punished for the crime. But a person forced to drink, or one hearing Islam while drunk and never again, is not eternally punished for it. |
| 3 | **Age of maturity (puberty)** | Islamic law treats puberty as adulthood. The lecturer argues modern norms have extended childhood. Children who die before maturity are not punished and remain in eternal bliss, **regardless of the religion of their parents**. |
| 4 | **The correct message must reach the person** | If only a distorted form reaches them, they are accountable only if they had access to correct information and interpretation. |
| 5 | **Sound senses** | Loss of senses (e.g. deaf-blind) that prevents understanding removes liability, varying by case. |
| 6 | **Ability to investigate the truth** | Not responsible beyond ability. Example: prisoners without access to information. |
| 7 | **Not under coercion threatening life or limb** | Mostly about acts (e.g. eating pork to survive). May also apply to belief conveyed under distress. |
| 8–10 | **Remaining conditions** | *Listed in the book; not detailed in the transcript.* |

### 15.1 Worked scenario (p. 165): John and Zayd
- **John:** "Your religion is violent; jihad is terrorism."
- **Zayd:** "Let me explain what jihad actually is."
- **John:** refuses to listen and has made up his mind.
- **Verdict:** John **is** mukallaf, since he has access to knowledge and deliberately bars himself from it. If he hears a correct explanation and still persists out of arrogance or bigotry, he bears responsibility.
- A medieval peasant who heard distortions is a different case, and Allah judges individually.

### 15.2 Common objections addressed
1. **"Is a child of a Christian or Hindu family condemned?"** No. A child is not condemned to the parents' faith if they die as a child.
   - Hadith that seem to say otherwise are treated as **abrogated** (*mansukh*), with a detailed discussion in the standard commentary.
2. **"Do people born Muslim have an unfair advantage?"** No. Salvation depends on the conditions and on free will, not just an outward profession.
   - A Muslim by outward profession could be a hypocrite (*munafiq*).
   - Some are religious fanatics lacking real understanding of belief.
   - A non-Muslim never reached by Islam is forgiven, and one who hears and accepts gains salvation.
3. **Groups vs. individuals:** the Qur'an declares groups (e.g. Christians, Jews who reject) to be in *kufr* with a promise of Hellfire, **but individuals within them may not fulfil the conditions of taklif**. The lecturer says this reflects the vastness of God's mercy.
4. **What can we say about the dead?** We judge by the outward (*"died a kafir"*) and leave ultimate judgment to Allah.

## 19. Revision Summary

**Chapter 3: Proofs of God's existence**
1. **Contingent** = exists after non-existence. The universe is contingent.
2. Anything that exists is **moving or still**. Neither can be eternal, since both involve change.
3. **Seven principles:** attributes exist beyond bodies, are not self-established, do not transfer, do not hide and reappear, the eternal cannot perish, bodies are never free of these attributes, and contingents cannot lack a first.
4. **Infinite regress of contingents is impossible**, shown by the chain argument and the tallying proof. Circular causation is also impossible.
5. **Possible things need a specifier.** The universe is possible, so it needs a **necessary, eternal, willing** cause.
6. **Krauss and Hawking** redefine "nothing" or invoke laws and multiverses, which are circular or regress the question.
7. **Common-sense first principles** (causality, sufficient reason, excluded middle, non-contradiction) are intuitive. Rejecting them is an **assault on reason**.
8. **Occam's razor** favours one eternal cause over a multiverse.
9. **Fallacy of composition** does not apply here, as the universe is a composite of contingents.
10. **God-of-the-gaps** is not a legitimate Muslim argument. **Alien-of-the-gaps** only regresses the question.

**Chapter 4: Problem of evil**
11. Evil must be **defined**, and only the Creator can define morality.
12. The atheist syllogism **imports human standards** and **ignores free will, divine names and the Hereafter**.
13. **Power** relates only to the possible, so the boulder question is fallacious.
14. **Knowledge** covers all three categories, and God's knowing does not coerce human acts.
15. **Taklif** has ten conditions, so judgments about individuals belong to God, and we judge only the outward.
