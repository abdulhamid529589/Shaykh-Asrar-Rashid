## 🟢 CHAPTER 3 — SECTION 3.4: The Contingency Argument
### *Page 110–112 of the Book*

### 3.4.1 — What Is Contingency? The Technical Definition

> *"What is meant by contingency is existence after non-existence. Therefore, anything which had no prior existence and then came into existence would be deemed as contingent."*

**The Proof's Three Technical Components:**

| Component | Content |
|---|---|
| **The Evidence** | The universe itself |
| **The Essence of the Evidence** | The contingent nature of the universe — that it came from non-existence |
| **The Direction of the Evidence** | The universe's neediness for an instantiating cause (*musabbib*) that brought it into being |
| **The Indication** | The impossibility of the universe coming into existence without an instantiating cause |

**The Three Ontological Categories (Critical Framework for all of Chapter 3):**

| Category | Arabic | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Possible** | *Mumkin / Ja'iz* | May or may not exist; existence and non-existence are equally conceivable | This table; the universe |
| **Necessary** | *Wajib al-Wujud* | Cannot NOT exist; its non-existence is a rational impossibility | Allah's existence |
| **Impossible** | *Mustahil* | Cannot exist; a logical contradiction | A circular triangle |

Everything that exists is either necessary or possible (contingent). There are no other options. The universe is clearly possible/contingent — it could have not existed. The question is what brought it from non-existence to existence.

---

### 3.4.2 — Why the Universe Is Contingent: The Movement and Stillness Sub-Argument

**The Minimum Premise:**
> *"At the very least, everything in the universe and indeed the universe itself is either moving or still."*

This is established by the **eye of the mind** (*'ayn al-'aql*) — rational intuition independent of physical observation. You do not need a telescope to know this.

**Why Both Movement and Stillness Are Contingent:**

*Movement as contingent:*
- Movement comes into existence after prior stillness — it was non-existent, then it existed
- When a body moves from point A to point B to point C, at each point it fills a different void
- Filling a new void is a **renewal** — a new contingent act at every stage

*Stillness as contingent:*
- We observe the universe moving — therefore at some point, stillness ended
- That which ends cannot be eternal
- Ending entails that the stillness had a beginning somewhere — which makes it contingent

**The Three-Position Analysis — Forcing the Opponent's Hand:**

Any atheist who insists the universe is eternal must accept one of these three positions:

**Position 1: The universe has been moving for eternity**

*The Problem:* A moving solid body (*jism*) — defined as anything that fills a void — at every moment of its movement fills a different void. Moving from void 1 to void 2 to void 3... entails renewal at every stage. This renewal is a contingent act. Therefore:

> *"An eternally moving material universe could not in fact be eternal — because even if we say the universe is moving continuously for eternity, a moving object at every given moment carries out a contingent act."*

Even eternal movement implies contingency at every moment. The universe cannot be simultaneously eternal and in perpetual movement.

**Position 2: The universe has been still for eternity**

*The Problem:* We observe movement in the universe. Therefore at some point, the universe went from stillness to movement. But:

> *"For an eternally still universe to suddenly move would mean that stillness is not actually eternal as it no longer exists. If stillness accepts perishing, then for its existence it needs a specifier as all that is possible can be either existent or non-existent."*

Stillness that ends is not eternal. Its ending means it was contingent.

**Position 3: The universe was neither moving nor still for eternity**

*The Problem:*

> *"The third proposal of universe and matter neither moving nor still is simply wrong because all matter always fills a void and when it fills a void it is either moving or still. This is understood by the eye of the mind."*

All matter by its very nature occupies space (fills a void). Whatever occupies space is either in motion or at rest. There is no third option for physical matter.

**Conclusion:** All three positions force the conclusion that the universe is contingent. There is no coherent way to maintain that the universe is eternal.

---

### 3.4.3 — The "Solid Body" Principle (Additional Caveat)

Shaykh Asrar introduces an important additional consideration regarding what he calls "solid bodies":

> *"Movement of solid bodies — meaning here anything that fills a void, whether a rock or light — moves from place to place. That would mean that when the moving material filled one void, it would stop at that void even momentarily and then move onto the next void, which would entail renewal at every stage of its movement, clearly implying its contingent nature."*

**This applies even to light (photons):** Even the fastest-moving physical entities, in passing from one location to another, "fill" successive voids in a sequence. Each moment of this sequential occupancy is a renewal — a contingent act. Therefore even at the most fundamental level of physical reality, continuous movement implies continuous contingency.

## 🔵 SECTION 3.5: The Seven Principles (*Usul*) — The Structural Foundation
### *Page 113–118 of the Book*

These seven principles are the **logical skeleton** of the contingency proof. They are found in classical kalam works — Imam al-Bajuri's famous commentary on the *Jawharat al-Tawhid* being the primary reference.

> *"Famously said: the seven gates of hell are shut for the one who knows these seven principles."*

The classical formulation means: understanding these seven principles safeguards one from fundamental philosophical errors about the nature of existence and the necessity of a creator.

---

### Principle 1 — Establishing Something Additional Existing to Solids

**What it means:** Any material thing (a *jism* — anything occupying space) has attributes **additional to itself**. The material thing is one thing; its attributes are something additional.

*Example: A planet*
- The planet exists
- The planet has a **shape** — additional to the planet
- The planet has **movement** (orbit) — additional to the planet
- The planet has a **color/composition** — additional to the planet

**The Philosophical Point:**
> *"You can never think of something that has no attributes. Even something that is clear and invisible — if you envision something being clear in your mind, the minimum attribute is that it is clear."*

This is foundational because the subsequent principles are all about the nature of these *additions* (attributes) and what their existence and contingency tell us about the material thing they belong to.

---

### Principle 2 — Invalidating the View That This Addition Is Self-Established

**What it means:** The additional attribute (e.g., movement) is **not self-standing** — it cannot exist independently of the material thing it belongs to.

*Planet example:* The orbit (movement) of Jupiter is not separable from Jupiter. You cannot have Jupiter's orbit without Jupiter. The orbit depends on the planet for its existence.

**The Philosophical Implication:** This means:
- The attribute cannot be its own cause
- The attribute requires the subject (the material body) to exist
- Therefore when we ask about the contingency of the attribute, we are really asking about the contingency of the body-plus-attribute combination

This prevents someone from arguing: "The movement of the universe always existed independently — therefore the universe is eternal."

---

### Principle 3 — Invalidating That This Addition Transfers

**What it means:** The attribute of one material body does not transfer to another material body.

*Planet example:* The movement (orbit) of Jupiter does not transfer itself to Earth. Each planet's movement is exclusive to that planet.

**Why This Matters:**

If attributes could transfer between objects, one might argue: "The universe's eternal movement transferred from one configuration to another — so the universe has always existed in some moving form." This principle closes that escape route. Each specific arrangement of attributes is particular to its subject.

---

### Principle 4 — Invalidating That the Addition Conceals and Then Reappears

**What it means:** If an attribute disappears (e.g., a planet stops moving), it does not go into "hiding" to reappear later. It genuinely ceases.

*Planet example:* If Jupiter stops orbiting, the orbit does not "conceal itself" and "reappear" when the planet moves again. The original orbit simply ended (contingent) and a new orbital movement came into being (also contingent).

**The Philosophical Implication:** This eliminates a Platonic-style escape: that attributes are eternal "forms" that merely manifest and hide. Every instance of an attribute is genuinely new — genuinely contingent.

---

### Principle 5 — The Impossibility of the Eternal Perishing

**What it means:** Something that is truly eternal cannot perish.

> *"The fifth principle is that the eternal does not accept perishing. If it did perish, it would mean that it is only possible and not necessary — and possibilities are contingent."*

**The Logical Form:**
```
IF something is eternal → it has no end → it cannot perish
IF something perishes → it had an end → it is not eternal → it is contingent
∴ Anything observed to perish is NOT eternal — it is contingent
```

**Application to the Universe:** We observe things in the universe perishing — stars collapse, galaxies change, matter transforms. These perishing things cannot be eternal.

---

### Principle 6 — Establishing That Material Bodies Never Separate from Their Additions

**What it means:** A material body (*jism*) is never without attributes. It always has some addition — shape, size, composition, position, movement, or stillness.

**The Implication:** Since material bodies always have additions (attributes), and since those attributes are contingent (they change, they began, they end), the material bodies themselves are always in a state of contingency-exhibiting existence. A material thing cannot exist in a "pure" state without any attribute — and its attributes are always contingent.

---

### Principle 7 — The Impossibility of Contingents with No First

**What it means:** There cannot be a chain of contingent things that goes back infinitely without a first link.

**The Object:** Someone says: "Fine — the universe is contingent. But maybe it has no beginning. It goes back infinitely in time."

**The Response:**

> *"Each component and individual of the everlasting chain of contingents would have a beginning. So because each component has a beginning, that would entail the entire chain has a beginning."*

**The Formal Reasoning:**
```
Consider a supposed infinite chain: ... → C → B → A → (present)
Each link in the chain (A, B, C...) has a beginning
Therefore the entire chain has a beginning
A chain whose every component has a beginning cannot itself be beginningless
∴ The chain must terminate at something that has NO beginning = the Necessary Being
```

**The Set Theory Objection and Response:**

> *"What is meant by numbers never ending is that you can continuously add in the mind and then multiply that number by the number of trees, stars and sand grains and then keep adding onto that number conceptually. But the eternal number will never have existence externally and neither could you actually comprehend it in the mind."*

Responding to someone who invokes Cantor's transfinite sets:

> *"As for what Caner termed as 'sets' — they are constructs of the mind that do not actually exist externally. Transfinite numbers cannot have an actual infinite existence. So too, sets with infinite cardinality cannot have an actual existence, because an infinite number of tasks can never be completed."*

**The distinction:** Conceptual (mental) infinity is possible — you can *think* of numbers going on forever. But **actual** infinite existence in the external world is impossible. No infinite chain of events has ever been completed, because completing an infinite series is a contradiction in terms.

> *"Anything that has a beginning, as with the number one — it is the beginning of a set of real numbers — added to infinitely, cannot be deemed as infinite. That which has a start and then is added to cannot be deemed as eternal in the way we ascribe to God."*

**Even Paradise (*Jannah*) Is Not Eternal in the Divine Sense:**

> *"The everlasting nature of paradise means that paradise had a start and is sustained by God to exist forever. This would mean that paradise is a contingent creation that will be sustained infinitely but cannot in any way be equated with God's *Azali* and eternal nature, as He has no beginning and no end and is not sustained by another."*

This is a crucial theological distinction: Paradise is *everlasting* (sempiternal — has a beginning but no end) while Allah's existence is *eternal* (no beginning and no end).

## 🔴 SECTION 3.6: Tasalsul (Infinite Regress) and Dawr (Circular Reasoning)
### *Page 116–121 of the Book*

### The Tally Proof (*Burhan al-Tatbiq*)

**Origin:** Found in Ibn Sina's *al-Shifa* (The Book of Healing) in the chapter on divinity. This proof demonstrates the impossibility of an actually infinite regress of contingent causes.

**The Proof:**

> *"For argument's sake, in the external realm, we argue for a case of chains of contingent events. Let's refer to this as chain of events that end at Z, and then we have another chain of events that finish at Y. The tallying is a mental exercise where you tally each event from one line of events with the other."*

```
Sequence 1: ... → E5 → E4 → E3 → E2 → E1 → Z (ends here)
Sequence 2: ... → E5 → E4 → E3 → E2 → E1 (ends here)

Sequence 1 exceeds Sequence 2 by exactly one event: Z
```

> *"The fact that Sequence 2 finishes means that it is not eternal. But most importantly, it would mean that Sequence 1 also comes to an end, because Sequence 1 has a limited increase to Sequence 2, and that limited increase is the point Z."*

**The Principle:**

> *"The Burhan proves that you cannot have an eternal regress of contingent events because if something increases, it entails that anything that increases by nature is not eternal — because the eternal by very definition is something that comes to no end."*

If Sequence 1 is only "longer" than Sequence 2 by one item (Z), and Sequence 2 is finite, then Sequence 1 is also finite. The addition of even one item to a finite sequence produces another finite sequence — never an actually infinite one.

**The General Conclusion:** Any chain of contingent events — however large — that can be compared and tallied is finite. Therefore no chain of contingent events can be actually infinite. There must be a first — and that first must be the Necessary Being.

---

### Impossibility of Infinite Regress (*Tasalsul*)

> *"Continuous regression means that if we say that the universe is contingent but was caused by, for instance, an alien life form — the question would arise as to what caused the alien life form. If the answer was again another alien life form, this would lead to a continuous regression of contingents. This is an impossibility since contingents by their very nature are instantiated ex nilo and therefore depend on an ultimate cause that is self-sufficient without beginning."*

**The Formal Statement:**
```
If contingent A was caused by contingent B
And contingent B was caused by contingent C
And contingent C was caused by contingent D... → no end

PROBLEM: The entire chain A+B+C+D+... is itself a set of contingents
That set of contingents requires a cause outside itself
If the cause is also contingent → regress continues
There is no grounding for actual existence
But things DO actually exist
∴ The regress must stop at a NON-contingent cause = Necessary Being
```

---

### Impossibility of Circular Reasoning (*Dawr*)

**The Definition:**
> *"Circular reasoning is also a rational impossibility. If it were proposed that A caused B and B also caused A — we would observe that the existence of A is dependent on B and likewise the existence of B is dependent on A."*

**The Two Types of Circularity:**

| Type | Example | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Conceptual circularity (valid)** | "Tom is father to John / John is son to Tom" | Valid — mutually defining concepts, not causal dependence |
| **Simultaneous causal relation (valid)** | Ball resting on pillow (depression and ball exist simultaneously); key turning with hand | Valid — simultaneous existence, not sequential causation |
| **Causal instantiation circularity (IMPOSSIBLE)** | "A caused B into existence AND B caused A into existence" | Impossible — both would have to *precede* each other in existence |

**The Hawking/Mlodinow Example of Circular Reasoning:**

From *The Grand Design*:
> *"Because there is a law of gravity, the universe can and will create itself out of nothing."*

**Why this is circular:**
> *"A law of nature depends for its own existence on the prior existence of the nature and material it describes. How can gravity — a law of nature — exist prior to the very inception of the universe? There is no physical law yet. They claim the physical law created the universe. A physical law that actually exists within the universe itself."*

The law of gravity presupposes the existence of matter and energy. You cannot invoke a law of nature to explain the origin of the nature to which that law belongs. This is precisely circular reasoning in instantiation.

**Another example from the same book:**
> *"M-theory predicts that a great many universes were created out of nothing. Their creation does not require the intervention of some supernatural being or god. Rather these multiple universes arise naturally from physical law."*

The same problem: "physical law" presupposes "physical reality." You cannot ground physical reality in physical law without already presupposing physical reality.

## 🟡 SECTION 3.7–3.9: The Proof from Possibility (*Burhan al-Imkan*)
### *Page 122–125 of the Book*

### The Quranic Starting Point

Shaykh Asrar references the Quranic verse:

> *"Has a time come upon humanity — a small given point of time — in which he was something not mentioned?"*

The rhetorical answer: Yes. There was a time when human beings did not exist. Now they exist. What brought them from non-existence to existence? Something preferred their existence. Something willed them into existence.

This is the heart of the *Burhan al-Imkan* — the Proof from Possibility.

---

### The Primary Syllogism:

```
PREMISE 1: The universe exists after it had not existed
           [Validated by the contingency discussion — movement/stillness,
            the seven principles, and Big Bang cosmology]

PREMISE 2: Every existent after prior non-existence has an originator
           that brings it into existence
           [Rational principle — the non-existent cannot originate itself;
            existence and non-existence are equally possible; a third
            party is needed to "tip the scale"]

CONCLUSION: The universe had an originator that brought it into existence
```

**The Atheist Objection to Premise 2:**

> *"What they will deny from Premise 2 is where it is stated that every existent after prior non-existence has an originator. They will say that is not necessary. You can have things coming into existence that do not actually require an originator."*

**The Islamic Response to this Objection:**

> *"That would be due to the impossibility of something originating itself and the fact that both its existence and non-existence would be equal in terms of probability, and for the non-existent to bring about its own existence is also a rational impossibility. Therefore, it would require something external and non-contingent to bring it into existence."*

**The Three-Step Logical Closure:**

1. The universe cannot originate itself — for it to create itself would mean it existed before it existed (contradiction)
2. If something external and contingent caused it → that contingent cause also needs a cause → regress (*Tasalsul*) → impossible
3. Therefore the cause must be external and **non-contingent** = the Necessary Being

---

### The Alternative Syllogism (The "Conditional" Form):

```
PREMISE 1: If the Creator of the universe did not exist by necessity,
           there would be no universe in existence

PREMISE 2: However, the universe DOES exist

CONCLUSION: Therefore, a Creator is necessarily existent
```

**The Elaboration — Three Options for the Cause:**

> *"The reasoning behind this is that the other two options for the creator would be that he is impossible or possible. Therefore *Qudra* is limited to what it can legitimately affect (possible things), while *'Ilm* has no such operational restriction.

---

### The Key Point for the Problem of Evil: Knowledge ≠ Compulsion

> *"What is essential to know is that Allah knowing does not mean that Allah strips away the free will from the human being. Allah knowing that this person will do this action does not mean Allah has enforced that action on the person. The person is still exercising his free will."*

**The Analogy:** A teacher who knows a student will fail their exam does not thereby *cause* the student to fail. The student's failure is their own choice. The teacher's foreknowledge and the student's free choice coexist without contradiction.

This resolves the apparent tension: divine foreknowledge (*'Ilm Azali*) and human free will (*Kasb*) are compatible.

### The Extrinsic vs. Intrinsic Distinction (Section 4.7)

> *"Sometimes the term 'rationally necessary' or 'rationally impossible' can become confused with what is extrinsically necessary or possible."*

| Type | Example | Can Allah Change It? |
|---|---|---|
| **Intrinsically impossible** | A circular triangle; 1+1=3 | No — these are pure logical contradictions with no existence in any universe |
| **Extrinsically impossible** | A tree growing without roots | Yes — "God could create a tree without roots, as the fundamental need of trees for roots is but a creation of God who made it necessary" |
| **Intrinsically necessary** | Allah's existence; 1+1=2 | No — these cannot be otherwise in any framework |
| **Extrinsically necessary** | The normal laws of physics | Yes — Allah can override them (miracles); different universes have different physical laws |

**The Ibn Arabi Reference:**

Ibn Arabi describes seeing things in *kashf* (spiritual vision) that appear to violate known laws — but these occurred in other "universes" or realms where different physical laws apply. This is distinct from logical impossibilities (circular triangles) which no universe can contain.

## 🟣 SECTION 4.8: Human Free Will (*Ikhtiyar* / *Kasb*)

### The Classical Islamic Position — "Soft Determinism":

> *"Human free will in accordance to the kalam methodology of Sunni rationalism relates to our limited choice that we have in the acquisition or earning of deeds."*

**Key Concepts:**

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Kasb / Iktisab* | Acquisition/earning — the human being's limited agency in bringing about their own actions within Allah's overarching decree |
| *Mukallaf* | The legally responsible person — one who is addressed by divine commands and accountable for their choices |
| *Jabr* | Absolute compulsion (rejected by Sunni theology) — the view that humans have no agency at all |
| *Qadar* | Divine decree — Allah's overarching will and determination |
| *Irada* | Will/volition — both divine and human; the human will is real but limited and operates within divine will |

**What "Soft Determinism" Means:**

> *"Some have referred to this as soft determinism — that in actual fact everything is determined by Allah. The human being has a scope of free will. That means everything is actually determined — and they refer to it as a soft determinism. But we would say that the will of a human being to exercise his limited will is his own choice."*

**The Problem of Evil Resolution via Free Will:**

Human evil — the majority of what we call "evil" in the world — results from humans misusing their free will. Allah gave humans a limited but genuine capacity to choose. The existence of this free will necessitates the possibility of its misuse. Without the possibility of misuse, there is no genuine freedom — and therefore no genuine moral virtue either (as Mufti Shamail argued in the debates: a test with only right answers is no test at all).

**Divine Knowledge and Free Will — Compatible:**

Allah knows eternally what every person will do. But knowing is not causing. The person's choice remains genuinely their own. This is the Sunni mainstream position distinguishing between divine foreknowledge (*Ilm Azali*) and divine compulsion (*Jabr*).

## 🟤 SECTION 4.9: The Ten Conditions of *Taklif* (Legal/Theological Responsibility)
### *Pages 162–169 of the Book*

This section is unique to this lecture in the entire course series. It addresses the question: **Who is actually held accountable before God?** — and the answer is far more nuanced than popular atheist or Muslim assumptions.

### Why This Section Matters:

> *"The ultimate judgment will be by God — not by bigots who think black and white, neither knowing the subtleties and the context nor what the Quran and the prophetic tradition actually state."*

The ten conditions of *taklif* explain why many of the people we meet daily may not be fully accountable — and why the mercy of Allah is far wider than a simplistic reading of Islamic theology suggests.

---

### The Ten Conditions:

**CONDITION 1 — Sound Intellect (*'Aql Salim*)**

> *"A person must have a sound intellect as opposed to being mentally insane or mentally ill, nor having any such fault of the mind which renders the person unable to rationalize belief."*

**Implications:**
- All people born with mental impairments, madness, or any inability of the mental faculties to comprehend divine commands will have salvation and not suffer punishment
- In Islamic law, the mentally insane are exempt from *hudud* (prescribed) punishments
- This same exemption applies to salvation — they are not condemned

**The Level of *Taklif* Matches the Level of *'Aql*:**

> *"The level of *taklif* is in accordance with the mind. Walk down our areas in Sparkbrook and other areas — you will see people who are not actually *mukallaf*. They cannot make a distinction between certain things. The level of *taklif* is less. It doesn't mean it disappears completely."*

**On a Person Who Was Sane Then Became Insane:**

> *"A person who was initially sane but who acquired misdeeds or became insane later — such an individual would be judged according to those misdeeds acquired during their time of sanity, given that he had not made amends prior to insanity."*

**CONDITION 2 — Age of Maturity (*Bulugh*)**

> *"A person must have reached the age of maturity. In Islamic law, a girl who reaches puberty — which can be from the age of nine — is deemed an adult. A young boy who reaches puberty is deemed an adult."*

**The Modern Context:**

> *"Because cultural norms changed with time, childhood is being extended. People's childhood is being extended — you have 28-year-olds playing PlayStation. Their childhood has been extended into their late 20s, never mind late 20s. But in Islam, in old ancient societies, people would mature very quickly mentally."*

**Children Who Die Before Maturity:**

> *"This means that children who die before maturity do not face perdition and in fact stay in eternal bliss."*

This applies across religious lines — Christian children, Hindu children, Buddhist children who die before puberty are in eternal bliss.

**On the Common Atheist Objection (Child's Religion):**

> *"Some atheists say: how can a child be described as a Hindu child or a Muslim child or a Buddhist child when a child is a child? The response is very simple — these children were born to parents who hold those respective beliefs. It does not in any way entail those children are to be condemned to the faith of their parents if they die upon their beliefs."*

**CONDITION 3 — The Correct Message Must Have Reached Them**

> *"If a distorted form of Islam reaches them, they are only deemed legally responsible if they had access to correct information and interpretation."*

**The Scenario:**

```
JOHN: "Your religion is violent because it has jihad = terrorism"
ZAID: "To equate jihad with terrorism is wrong — let me explain..."
JOHN: "I don't want to hear. I've made up my mind."
```

In this case, John is *mukallaf* — he has access to correct information and is deliberately refusing it. His refusal is his own choice and he bears responsibility.

**The Medieval Peasant Scenario:**

People who lived in medieval Europe and heard Islam only through the lens of Crusader propaganda — their judgment is with Allah, dependent on what actually reached them and their capacity to investigate.

> *"Every individual who hears of Islam — taking into account the other conditions — is legally responsible to learn the facts if they have access to correct information. An outright refusal to hear the truth despite being able to do so would render the person culpable."*

**CONDITION 4 — Sound Senses**

> *"If the loss of any sense impairs their understanding of what is being conveyed, they are not deemed liable for not adopting the true message. This would include someone born deaf and blind."*

This condition varies in accordance with the type of sensory disability. Partial sensory disability reduces (but does not necessarily eliminate) responsibility.

**CONDITION 5 — Ability to Investigate the Truth**

> *"If this does not exist due to a lack of comprehension and mental inability to do so, or physical constraints like being a prisoner, for example, then the person will not be legally responsible other than according to their ability."*

Example: A person who spends their entire life in prison without access to any Islamic information. Their judgment — only Allah knows. They cannot be straightforwardly condemned.

**CONDITION 6 — Absence of Coercive Situation**

> *"A person is not in a coercive situation which threatens life or limb. An example would be eating pork to stay alive when no other food is available, or having a swig of alcohol to wash down a morsel that is choking a person to death, it being the nearest available drink."*

In states of extreme duress, the normal rules of legal responsibility do not fully apply.

### For Section 3.4–3.5 (Contingency and Seven Principles):
- **Imam al-Bajuri — *Hashiyat al-Bajuri 'ala Jawharat al-Tawhid*** — The primary text for the Seven Principles; essential classical kalam
- **Ibrahim al-Laqqani — *Jawharat al-Tawhid*** — The poem being commented upon; memorized by traditional scholars
- **Shaykh Asrar Rashid's lectures on *Umm al-Barahin*** — Referenced in the lecture as available online
- **Al-Sanusi — *Umm al-Barahin* (*al-'Aqida al-Sanusiyya*)** — Another essential kalam text; covers the 20 attributes and their proofs

### For Section 3.6 (Tasalsul and Dawr):
- **Ibn Sina — *al-Shifa* (The Book of Healing)**, Chapter on Divinity — Contains the *Burhan al-Tatbiq*
- **Ibn Sina — *Kitab al-Najat*** — More accessible summary of his philosophical theology
- **Al-Ghazali — *Tahafut al-Falasifa* (The Incoherence of the Philosophers)** — His critique of Ibn Sina's philosophical theology
- **Ibn Rushd — *Tahafut al-Tahafut* (The Incoherence of the Incoherence)** — Response to al-Ghazali

### For Section 3.7–3.9 (Proof from Possibility):
- **The Kalam Cosmological Argument (William Lane Craig)** — Modern Western formulation; his book *The Kalam Cosmological Argument* (1979)
- **Leibniz — *Monadology* and *Theodicy*** — The Principle of Sufficient Reason in full philosophical development
- **Al-Ghazali — *Ihya' 'Ulum al-Din***, Book of Belief — The Islamic version of the proof from possibility

### For Section 3.10 (Lawrence Krauss):
- **Lawrence Krauss — *A Universe from Nothing* (2012)** — Read the book; then read David Albert's critical review in the New York Times (March 2012) — one of the most important philosophical critiques
- **Stephen Hawking and Leonard Mlodinow — *The Grand Design* (2010)** — The circular reasoning Shaykh Asrar identifies
- **John Lennox — *God and Stephen Hawking*** — A direct philosophical response to The Grand Design

### For Section 3.11–3.13 (Fallacy of Composition / Russell / Hume):
- **David Hume — *Dialogues Concerning Natural Religion*** — The primary source of Hume's critique of the cosmological argument
- **Bertrand Russell and F.C. Copleston — BBC Radio Debate (1948)** — Available in transcript and audio; the "brute fact" argument
- **Immanuel Kant — *Critique of Pure Reason***, Transcendental Dialectic — His treatment of the cosmological argument
- **Alvin Plantinga — *God and Other Minds* (1967)** — The most rigorous philosophical response from a Western analytic tradition

### For Section 3.18–3.19 (God of the Gaps / Alien of the Gaps):
- **Francis Crick — *Life Itself: Its Origin and Nature* (1981)** — The panspermia book
- **Richard Dawkins — *The God Delusion* (2006)**, Chapter 4 — His "Alien of the Gaps" interview is in *Expelled: No Intelligence Allowed* (2008 documentary)
- **Fazale Rana and Hugh Ross — *Origins of Life*** — Scientific critique of abiogenesis from a theistic perspective

### For Section 3.20 (Anthony Flew):
- **Anthony Flew — *There Is a God: How the World's Most Notorious Atheist Changed His Mind* (2007)** — His intellectual journey
- **Roy Abraham Varghese's appendix in *There Is a God*** — Elaborates on Integrated Complexity

### For Chapter 4 (Problem of Evil):
- **Alvin Plantinga — *God, Freedom, and Evil* (1974)** — The Free Will Defence; the most rigorous philosophical response to the logical problem of evil
- **John Hick — *Evil and the God of Love* (1966)** — The "Soul-Making Theodicy" (a Christian response, useful comparison)
- **Al-Ghazali — *Ihya' 'Ulum al-Din***, Chapter on Patience (*Sabr*) — Islamic perspective on suffering and its meaning
- **Steven Fry's Interview (RTE, 2015)** — Watch the original interview to understand what is being analyzed
- **Arthur Conan Doyle — "The Adventure of the Cardboard Box" and "The Veiled Lodger"** — The Holmes quotes that open Chapter 4

### For Section 4.3–4.7 (*Qudra*, *'Ilm*, Boulder Question):
- **Classical *Aqida* texts** — The 20 attributes of Allah with proofs; al-Bajuri's *Hashiya* is most comprehensive
- **Shaykh Asrar Rashid's lectures on *Umm al-Barahin*** — Direct coverage of *Qudra* and its scope
- **J.L. Mackie — "Evil and Omnipotence" (1955)** — The classic philosophical formulation of the Problem of Evil (the "Logical Problem")

### For Section 4.8–4.9 (Free Will and *Taklif*):
- **Al-Mawardi — *al-Ahkam al-Sultaniyya*** — Classical text on Islamic law including *taklif* conditions
- **Ibn Hajar al-'Asqalani — *Fath al-Bari*** — Commentary on Bukhari; discussions on children's salvation and related hadiths
- **Al-Ghazali — *Ihya' 'Ulum al-Din***, Book on Divine Knowledge and Qadar — Free will and divine decree
- **Compatibilism (Philosophy)** — The general philosophical framework for free will existing alongside determinism
